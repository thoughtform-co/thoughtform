# Rubric: Interface kit — the design grid, graded

Grade every candidate before a human sees it, against the shipped control of
the same subject. Read each still **as someone who has never seen the brief**,
with the real panel on the desk beside them.

**Version 0.1, 2026-09-05, uncalibrated. Reporting only.** Written from the
brand law and from one measurement pass over two references, before any owner
verdict exists. Nothing here gates. Every check reports, `tools/qa.py` prints
and refuses to decide, and the ladder below is the shape the instrument will
take once the first wave has said where it disagrees with the person running
it.

The grader parses the tables below at runtime and holds no copy of them. Four
columns: id, check, fails when, severity. Severity is `gate` (any failure kills
the asset), `critical` (one failure means redraw), `minor` (two mean redraw, one
is a note) or `advisory` (graded and reported, never counted). A block whose
heading contains _set level_ is graded across the wave, never on one frame.
**Edit checks here, never in the script.**

⚠ **THIS SHIP GRADES A RENDERED INTERFACE, NOT A GENERATED PHOTOGRAPH**, and
that reverses one of the harness's own defaults. `qa.py`'s built-in preamble
calls the second image an identity reference whose subject is the truth and any
deviation a fault — correct when the candidate is a drawn photograph of a real
object, and wrong here, where image 2 is the CURRENT DESIGN and the candidate is
an argument with it. The `## Grading rules` section below overrides that
preamble verbatim, and it is the first thing to check if a run starts scoring
every direction down for not being the control.

## Grading rules

The grader reads this section into its prompt verbatim.

- **The candidate is a screenshot of a live interface panel inside its own HUD
  frame**, at the viewport and theme its subject names. Nothing in it was
  generated by a model, and there is no photographic subject to be accurate to.
- **Image 2 is the SHIPPED CONTROL of the same viewport and theme.** It is the
  house's current baseline for scale, density and register. It is **not identity
  truth**: a difference from it is a design decision to be judged by the checks
  below, never a fault by itself. Say what changed and whether the check likes
  it.
- **Count before you judge.** Most of these checks are arithmetic. Count the
  elements drawn in the bright accent colour. Count the distinct line weights.
  Count the visibly different letter-spacings among labels of one rank. Count
  the things set bolder than their neighbours.
- **Absence is the usual miss.** Graders over-penalise excess and under-penalise
  absence — look for the rule that is NOT there: a panel edge with no line under
  it, a region with no seam, a label with no rung.
- **Judge contrast against the ground the theme actually shows**, not against an
  imagined dark page. A light still is judged on parchment.
- **Judge what the still shows.** A part outside the framing passes its check.
- **Fail when unsure**, and say in the note what you could not see.

## Stranger's read

One call that sees only the candidate, with no direction name and no reference,
before the rubric is applied. A prompt that names the surface gets the surface's
name back whatever is on screen, which is why this is a separate call. The
grader reads the fenced text verbatim.

```
You are shown one screenshot of an interface. Name what kind of screen it is in
one short noun phrase, as you would to someone who has not seen it, with no
brand names and no negations. Then say in one sentence what the person looking
at it is meant to do. Then count the distinct panels or regions you can see, and
count the elements drawn in the brightest accent colour.
```

## A. The accent. Any failure here stops the still.

| ID  | Check                                                                                                                                                                              | Fails when                                                                                         | Severity |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| A1  | **The accent is spent, not sprayed.** Count the elements drawn in the bright accent. A reader should be able to say what each one is for: where I am, what is open, what to press. | more than eight accent-coloured elements compete, or one cannot be given a job                     | gate     |
| A2  | **The accent does not draw structure.** Dividers, seams, panel edges and the boxes around marks belong to the neutral ink.                                                         | a rule, a divider or a box outline is drawn in the accent, the housing's own machined lip excepted | critical |
| A3  | **Selection is elaboration, not a hue change.** The chosen item earns more information or a filled mark while its siblings keep their colour.                                      | selection is signalled only by a colour swap, or is invisible among its siblings                   | critical |
| A4  | **No hue outside the house's tiers.** Neutral ground, neutral ink, one accent, one provenance green, one alert.                                                                    | a third hue, a cool tint, or a purple/blue gradient appears                                        | gate     |

## B. The type

| ID  | Check                                                                                                       | Fails when                                                                               | Severity |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| B1  | **Two faces, by role.** The chrome face carries labels, keys and data; the prose face carries sentences.    | a sentence is set in the chrome face, a label in the prose face, or a third face appears | gate     |
| B2  | **Nothing above the UI weight ceiling.** Emphasis is ink, not bold. A display title may be heavier.         | a label, a row, a tab, a key or a claim is set bolder than its neighbours for emphasis   | minor    |
| B3  | **Labels of one rank share one tracking rung.**                                                             | two labels at the same rank are visibly differently tracked                              | minor    |
| B4  | **Case ranks something.** Uppercase belongs to the chrome face; prose and display titles are sentence case. | everything is uppercase, so case says nothing, or a sentence is shouted                  | critical |
| B5  | **Nothing is under its floor.** Controls read at ten pixels or more; decorative chrome at eight and a half. | any string is too small to read at the still's own scale                                 | critical |

## C. The structure

| ID  | Check                                                                                                                                | Fails when                                                        | Severity |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| C1  | **One line weight for structure, in one hue,** with at most three alphas by role.                                                    | a fourth weight appears, or two rungs cannot be told apart        | critical |
| C2  | **The panel is seated.** Its edges and its internal seams land on the frame's own rules and ticks rather than floating between them. | an edge or a seam floats with nothing behind it                   | critical |
| C3  | **Square corners; one chamfered object per depth; its children square.**                                                             | any rounded corner, or a chamfer nested inside a chamfer          | gate     |
| C4  | **The lawful diagonal.** Where a pair of marks registers a composition, it is top-right and bottom-left.                             | a mark pair runs the other diagonal without being a mirrored back | minor    |

## D. The material

| ID  | Check                                                                                                                                   | Fails when                                                                               | Severity |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| D1  | **Flat.** One ground, one step lighter for a bar. No gradient ramp as decoration, no bloom, no glow.                                    | a ramp, a halo or a soft glow is doing the work a line should do                         | critical |
| D2  | **Contrast holds in the theme shown**, composited on the ground behind it.                                                              | a label or a rule is too faint to read against what is actually behind it                | critical |
| D3  | **Theme parity.** The light still re-derives its alphas and takes the accent's line and ink steps rather than inheriting the dark ones. | pale-on-accent text, a dark scrim on a light page, or an alpha that vanished in the flip | critical |

## E. The read

| ID  | Check                                                                                                  | Fails when                                                                             | Severity |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| E1  | **One first read.** Name the claim from across the room.                                               | nothing leads, or three things lead together                                           | minor    |
| E2  | **Nothing said twice.**                                                                                | a string appears in two places on one still                                            | minor    |
| E3  | **Instrument, not poster.** It should read as the device's own chrome rather than as a picture of one. | anything is decorative, or the stranger's read names a poster, a slide or a photograph | advisory |

## F. Set level

| ID  | Check                                                                                                                          | Fails when                                                                          | Severity |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| F1  | **Each direction differs on the axis it claims,** and on no other.                                                             | two directions' stills are indistinguishable, or one moves an axis it did not claim | critical |
| F2  | **One grammar across a direction's six stills.** A direction is a rule, and a rule holds at every viewport and in both themes. | a direction reads as two different instruments across its own set                   | critical |

## Scoring

Mirrored by `tools/config.py`'s verdict map. Change both together or not at all.

| Result                                     | Verdict                                     |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| Any gate failure                           | FAIL. Never keep. Redraw.                   |
| Any critical failure, or two or more minor | RETRY.                                      |
| Exactly one minor failure                  | PASS_WITH_NOTES. Keepable after an eyeball. |
| Clean                                      | PASS.                                       |

Rank keepers strictly: PASS beats PASS_WITH_NOTES beats RETRY. Two draws with
equal scores means the rubric did not discriminate — never promote from a tie.

## Calibration anchors

**Empty of approvals, and deliberately not empty of ground truth.** An anchor is
an output the owner has approved, promoted byte-identical into `skill/assets/`
with the dated quote that approved it. No verdict exists yet.

What the CONTROL stills are, and are not: they are **the current baseline** —
image 2 on every grade, and the thing F1 and F2 are read against. They are **not
approved anchors**, and the rubric expects the control itself to fail A1, B2 and
B3, because that is what the measurement found and what this whole wave is
about. **A rubric that its own baseline passes on every check is a rubric that
cannot say anything.**

## The harness

`tools/qa.py` parses the check tables above at runtime and holds no copy of
them. The one hardcoded piece is the verdict tier map in `tools/config.py`,
which mirrors the Scoring table.

**Grade three times, report the majority.** `--runs` defaults to 3, ties fail,
and every candidate whose verdict was unstable across runs is listed by name.

⚠ **AND THE CANDIDATES ARE DETERMINISTIC HERE, WHICH THE HARNESS IS NOT USED
TO.** Two runs of the capture produce the same pixels, so every point of spread
in a verdict is the GRADER's, not the subject's. That makes the unstable list
unusually informative on this ship: it is a direct read on which checks are
written badly enough to be a coin flip, and it is the first thing to fix in 0.2.

**Calibration status: uncalibrated, reporting only.**

## Regression ritual

Once a direction is approved, name three stills: the winner at the binding
viewport, the winner at the owner's own 1920×1247, and the control. After any
change to the kit's tokens, re-shoot those three, grade them, and compare
against `eval-log.md`. **If they get worse, the change reverts.**
