**2026-09-05 · rubric 0.1 · uncalibrated, reporting only**

## Setup

The first wave on this ship, and the first time the harness has graded something
nobody drew. Sixty candidates: ten directions × three viewports × two themes,
each one a Playwright still of `/test/interface-kit?view=panel` at a knob set,
shot by `scripts/capture-interface-kit.mjs` in the site repo. Six control stills
from `mount=shipped` — the real `ServicesCasefile` — are the subjects' identity
references, so every candidate was graded and reviewed beside the shipped panel
at its own viewport and theme.

## The finding

**The rubric fails its own baseline on A1, and that is the wave working.** The
control — the shipped panel, recomposed byte-for-byte in its boxes — is graded
FAIL for spending the accent too many times to mean anything. That is the
measurement this whole lab was built on, arrived at independently by a grader
that was never shown the number.

**A2 is written wrong, and KH and KI are what proved it.** Five of the six
non-A1 failures are "a structural line is drawn in the accent" on the two
directions whose entire proposal is to mark a selected station with an accent
line instead of a fill. A selection marker is a MARK, not structure — the rubric
knows the difference in prose and A2's wording does not. This is a rubric
defect, not a design defect, and it is the first row of 0.2's repair history:
**A2 must exempt a mark that carries state.** Left as written for now, because
a check edited before the wave that paid for it is a check nobody can trace.

**Thirty-one of sixty are unstable across three runs.** On this ship that number
means more than usual: the candidates are DETERMINISTIC — two captures produce
the same pixels — so every point of spread is the grader's, not the subject's.
Half the rubric is a coin at 0.1. The three directions that came back 6/6 (KC,
KD, KE) are the three whose axis is countable from a still; the ones that
wobbled are the ones asking a compositional question. That is a useful split and
it says where 0.2 should spend its words.

**And the stranger's read never once said "instrument".** Sixty naive reads,
and every one of them is some variant of _dashboard_ — "workflow intelligence
dashboard", "interactive case study dashboard", "tactical workflow dashboard".
Not one console, panel or control surface. It reads the same on the control as
on the composite, so it is a property of the surface rather than of a direction,
and it is the most interesting thing in the wave: the register the house is
aiming for is not the register a first-time viewer receives.

## What changed because of it

Nothing in the rubric yet, deliberately. Two things changed in the CAPTURE, both
found by looking at a still rather than by a gate:

- **The panel sat 145px off its seat** at every viewport. `.ik-proof-stage` was
  `position: absolute; inset: 0`, and an absolutely positioned child resolves
  against the containing block's PADDING box — so the stage spanned the whole
  band and handed `.fl-case` a containing block starting at zero. ⚠ **The
  KA-versus-control box gate passed the whole time**, because both stills were
  rendered inside the same wrong box: a parity check between two things broken
  the same way reports parity. The new gate asks the layout law instead —
  `.fl-case` must sit at the stage's content edge plus the instrument inset plus
  the housing pad — which is independent of anything else in the lab.
- **And that gate was itself wrong on its first run**, reporting 48px of error
  at 1920×1247 only. `--instrument-inset` is a `calc()` of three clamps, so
  `getPropertyValue` returns the expression and `parseFloat` returns NaN, which
  coerces to zero and makes the check agree with itself below the instrument
  tier and disagree above it. Resolved through a probe element now: **a custom
  property is a string until something lays it out.**
- **`grid=ruled` was drawing invented structure.** Repeating vertical rules at a
  third of the record column's width, corresponding to nothing, striping through
  the brief. The grader named it on the first pass ("misplaced grid overlay
  across the left pane"). It rules the margins now — the rail's thirteen rungs
  as stubs at both band edges, and the column split run full height — lines the
  panel already lands on rather than lines drawn behind the copy.

## Ruled out

- **Forcing a tracking rung onto the map.** Its labels are placed by arithmetic
  against `MONO_ADVANCE`, which includes the drawing's own tracking; a CSS
  override would move every glyph the projection already solved for and overflow
  cells no test would catch, because the tests check the model rather than the
  render. Four of the panel's rungs are the map's and joining them is a change
  to `mapProjection`, not to a stylesheet. It is why `track=tight` reads 7 and
  not 1.
- **Keeping gold on the selected cartridge under `accent=budget`.** One token
  strokes both the configured and the open state; they differ only by a fill
  alpha of 0.07 against 0.18. So the accent is spent on all twenty and marks
  none of them, and no lab rule can separate them — a second token in `pda.css`
  is the honest fix, and it is production work, not lab work.
- **Grading F1 and F2 in the harness.** They are set-level and the contact sheet
  is where they are read; the run says so on every pass.
