# ADR-103: The head decodes in place, and the plates become the deliverables

**Status:** Proposed (2026-09-15) — built and guarded, pending the owner's live read.
**Surface:** `#proposition` on `/arcs/trinny-london/proposal` (ADR-093 → ADR-094 → ADR-099 → ADR-100 → ADR-101 → ADR-102), which now holds a THIRD beat; the `steps` section kind on the arcs surface.
**Extends:** ADR-102 (the pinned scene — every window below is appended to its table; nothing before `SCENE_END` 3.18 moves except the head, which no longer closes), ADR-052 (a fifth enumerated exception to "new arcs are content-only").
**Related:** ADR-101 §B (one material — the rows are the bands), ADR-089 U4 (the open one filled, the rest outlined), ADR-097 U12 (pure motion, zero fades), ADR-078 U1 (draw the record), ADR-099 U2 (the head datum the three heads share).

## The ask

Owner, 2026-09-15, scoping the next stage after the Linear-style heads landed:

> When you scroll from the "Studio Today and Configured" section into the next section (a modular approach that compounds the H1 and paragraph texts), the little eyebrow thing above it disappears. I think they should just use a glitch effect to morph that way. The user sees that the section actually stays the same because the hero one and the paragraph always appear at the same location. I think it makes more sense to just morph it via a glitch effect than to have them disappear … and then reappear again in the next section.

And a new beat after the phases — the deck's "What we help build." slide — "not a simulacrum of those three modules because those are the offerings. This is more about what you actually get: a creative tech setup, a self-sufficient team, and a system that works and is independent". The three module cards "collapse a bit and then move to the left, vertically stacked" into a carousel (CloudForce's `Build. Migrate. Maintain.`, HEX's stepped rows: one lit, the rest dim) with "some sort of sticky effect so that when you scroll, you don't scroll to the next section but just scroll through the three parts"; on the right "a clean visual that really represents each of these three deliverables" in the house's diagram language, the first being "one of the images we've generated, along with some sort of super simple computer vision animation" that "scans the image and then has labels for colors, product consistency, etc." (reference: sutera.ch). Placeholders for the other two; copy to refine later.

## The diagnosis

1. **The head had a dead band, and the eyebrow was the witness.** ADR-102 closed the board's two head columns on WITHDRAW (0.40–0.70) and opened the phases' on TITLE (1.34–1.64) and PARAGRAPH (2.78–3.02). The three heads are pixel-identical on the datum (ADR-100 U4 measured title x 360 / copy x 1146.7), so the title and paragraph read as staying while the frame carried no head at all for 0.64 viewports and no right column for 2.08 — and the eyebrow's two strings differ, so it was the one thing that visibly went and came back. Nothing hid it; it was a passenger of its column's aperture.
2. **"Glitch" on this site is the decode kernel, not a flash.** ADR-097 U12 pulled the luminance glitch as a photosensitivity risk; what survives as "the glitch effect on our homepage" is `captionScramble`'s text decode — already scrubbed and reversible on this route's turn (`turnDecode`) and its carriers (`seamDecode`). The head morph is that kernel with zero travel.
3. **The third beat must live inside the scene.** The bands travel from the plates to the rows; a hand-over across a station boundary is the document-space carrier ADR-102 retired for its jitter. Everything is appended to one clock.

## The decision

### A. One head, decoded in place by a carrier layer (`turn/headCarrier.ts`)

- The board's head STANDS through the fold. The WITHDRAW aperture keeps only the ledger svg; the four head-column aperture rules and their `--tl-ap-title` / `--tl-ap-intro` channels are deleted.
- A second layer in the stage, `.tl-head`, mounted from `measure()` beside `.tl-seam`, holds one leaf per RENDERED LINE of each head run — the eyebrow, the title's `pre` (a bare Text node) and `em`, the coord stamp on the lead column; the designator, the paragraph and its stamp on the intro column. Lines are found by walking the run word by word with a `Range` and grouping rects by line; each leaf is posed on its line's own pixels in the stage's coordinates, with the run's computed face copied INLINE (family, size, weight, line-height, tracking, transform, colour, shadow) and `white-space: nowrap`. A `Range` rect is the content area, so the leaf's top takes the half-leading off (`top − (lineHeight − rect.height) / 2`).
- Windows `SCENE_HEAD_LEAD = [[1.34, 1.64], [3.34, 3.64]]` and `SCENE_HEAD_INTRO = [[2.78, 3.02], [4.12, 4.36]]` — ADR-102's title and paragraph timing kept for the first pair (the title as band 1 lands, the paragraph after the third plate), the second pair over the collapse and after the third band lands. `headState(col, sv)` runs `null → "decode" → "1" → "decode" → "2"`, stamped on `#proposition` as `data-tl-head-lead` / `data-tl-head-intro`; the sheet hides the columns' TEXT children per beat (never `.arc-head__lead` or `.arc-head`, which carry chrome and the arrival keyframe), and beat 0's chrome (grid, crosses) serves all three heads. Absent stamp = beat 0's text shown.
- **The decode rides `decodeClock`, the seam's own hold** — the window is when the layer paints, but the text it paints is beat k's, byte-equal, through the window's first fifth and beat k + 1's through its last tenth (ADR-102's trap, one layer over: a decode from zero shuffles the band it stands on — at `e` 0.03 the wall had already made `Trinny` into `HEIIny`, and the frame the real text went out on carried four wrong letters).
- **Register law:** mono chrome and the display title SCRAMBLE (`seamDecodeFrame`, one wall per column carrier); the paragraph TYPES (`typeCounts` + `typedSlice`: the outgoing run un-typed from its end, the incoming typed from its start, ONE typewriter across its lines — the first cut typed each line in parallel and read as three cursors). `ArcDecodeText`'s own ruling: the caps glyph pool reads as noise through lowercase prose.
- Welds: at a window's start the leaves are byte-equal to beat k's text on beat k's pixels; at its end byte-equal to beat k + 1's on the same pixels. The smoke measures the leaf against the real line's `Range` rect to 0.5px on all four ends.

### B. The third beat: `steps` (`components/arcs/ArcSteps.tsx`, `ArcScanVisual.tsx`)

- **The kind** — `{ head, items: [{ id, kicker, name, body, visual }] }`, the fifth enumerated exception (dossier · configuration · flow · board · steps): server, no state, no listener, `data-steps-*` only, dispatched in BOTH `ArcSectionRenderer` and `TrinnyBeats`, `KIND_DESIG.steps = "OUTCOMES"`. Rows are `.arc-plate`s with only a head band and a body line — the plates' own material, so the bands can become them with no change of material (ADR-101 §B's one-material law). Every motion channel is a custom property with a FINISHED default (`--tl-row-h` absent = `auto`, `--tl-lit` absent = 1, `--tl-vis` absent = 1, `--scan-s` absent = 1), so the static render, no-JS and reduced motion read every row open and filled, every stage shown, the scan complete.
- **The record** — `TRINNY_OUTCOMES` (id `outcomes`): eyebrow `what you get`, title `What we help / build.` (his own slide title), the deck's lede, three items — `01 · The setup` / **A creative tech setup**, `02 · The team` / **A team that runs it themselves**, `03 · The system` / **No long-term dependency** — with bodies drawn from the deck and Suri's `what-we-build` cards, noun-swapped. Draft; his to rewrite. `trinny-offer` pins three items, names ≤ 40, kickers ≤ 24, bodies ≤ 190 / ≤ 3 sentences, and that NO row restates a phase's name or deliverable line.
- **The choreography** (`turnClock.ts`, svh, appended): COLLAPSE `[[3.40, 3.64], [3.32, 3.56], [3.24, 3.48]]` — LIFO, `unrollY` runs back to `headH` (`ramp(UNROLL) − ramp(COLLAPSE)`, reversible); `plateState` gains two states (`unroll` again, then `held` from its carrier's birth); STACK — carriers 3, 4, 5 (`SCENE_CARRY[3..5]` = `[3.68, 3.98] / [3.74, 4.04] / [3.80, 4.10]`), each born on plate i's band and landing on row i's head (band 1 widens in place; 2 and 3 travel left and down), words decoding on `decodeClock`; `rowState` HELD until its band lands; STEPS from `SCENE_STEP_START` 4.40 at `SCENE_STEP_SPAN` **0.5** (the one dial) — `stepWeight` partitions the frame with a `SCENE_STEP_CROSS` 0.16 geometric crossover at each boundary, `stepOpen` drives a row's body (`--tl-row-h`, band → whole) AND its stage's centre-out aperture (`--tl-vis`), `rowLit` holds every band lit until the stepping starts (the weld is one material) then dims the closed rows to ring-only (ADR-089 U4), `stepProgress(i)` is each stage's own clock (0 before its step, 1 after, never rewinding under a closing aperture); `SCENE_END` 5.98, runway **605svh**.
- **The stage** — `.arc-steps__stages` is one definite box (`--arc-steps-h: clamp(240px, 42svh, 560px)`) inside the scene; the three stages sit over each other, each clipped `inset(0 calc(50% − vis·50%))`, the closing one shrinking to the slit while the next grows over it in DOM order. `data-tl-step="0|1|2"` on `#proposition`.
- **The scan** (stage 1) — a framed 4:5 field (opaque `--arc-plate` bed + the 24px `--arc-grid` matrix + dashed hairline runs + four registration crosses, the head's own mark), the packshot twice from one src (a grey ghost under, the live copy clipped to the sweep), a 1px `--gold-line` edge travelling top → bottom that parks as the verdict's rule, four callouts as the edge passes their anchor — a 7×7 open node on the image, one horizontal 1px `--arc-ink-60` div leader, a label box on its own bed — `WORDMARK · IN PLACE, LEGIBLE` · `COLOUR · ON THE PALETTE` · `PRODUCT · MATCHES THE PACKSHOT` · `LIGHT · MATCHES THE SET` — and `PASS · TO THE STUDIO LEAD`. The checks are the ones the studio's grading gates; composition is left to the human on purpose (the offer's own copy). Every reveal is a travelling or centre-out clip, no opacity, no transition, no keyframe. Two fixes above the field (`NAKED AMBITION · PACKSHOT` / `GRADING PASS`); the right one hides under `@container (max-width: 520px)` (they collided at the laptop's ~200px field). Stages 2 and 3 hold the same frame with one centred node over a designation.

## Traps this pass met

1. **Per-line typing reads as three typewriters.** Typing each paragraph line in parallel showed `The process / work is buil / fruit` at once. The count runs over the whole run; each line slices its own offset.
2. **The head carrier's comment tripped the seam guard's source pin** (`/scrollY/` matched the prose). The pins read the file as text; say "document-space scroll offset".
3. **The two mono fixes collide on a narrow figure** — a 200px field cannot carry two 8px runs; the figure is a size container, so a container query hides the right one.
4. **`getBoundingClientRect` on a clipped plate reports the box, not the clip** — the walk's `plates` heights read 497 through the collapse; the clip's `y` (`--tl-unroll-y`) is the state.
5. **The scene stamp is the clock to two places, and the roll converges to 0.01** — `rollToS` stops within a hundredth, so a converging roll at 1440×800 stamped `3.09` against the feet case's `"3.10"` string. Compare the number at the roll's own tolerance, never the stamp's serialisation.
6. **A covering beat that paints nothing still takes every hit test.** `#outcomes` is laid over the whole stage from the pin, and the ADR-098 corner probe found it at all four corners of plate 1 with every row held and every stage shut. ADR-101 §A's awaiting-station ruling, one beat later: `#proposition[data-tl-scene]:not([data-tl-step]) #outcomes` is `pointer-events: none` — the step stamp is the writer's word that the reader is stepping through the beat.
