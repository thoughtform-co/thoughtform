# ADR-102: One pinned scene — the configuration collapses into its chip, and the chip becomes the plates

**Status:** Proposed (2026-09-14) — built and guarded, pending the owner's live read.
**Surface:** `#proposition` on `/arcs/trinny-london/proposal` (ADR-093 → ADR-094 → ADR-099 → ADR-100 → ADR-101), which now holds the phases beat too.
**Supersedes on this page:** ADR-101 §A.3 (the seam clock `t` and the phases' strike) and §B (the document-space carrier and its three-window sum); ADR-099's "the configuration is not pinned" — it is, again, for a reason ADR-099's own finding allows.
**Related:** ADR-101 (the arrival strike, untouched), ADR-097 U12 (the aperture skin), ADR-100 (the board the scene folds), ADR-098 U2/U5 (the plates the chip becomes), ADR-021 (the motion law), the hero curtain's measurement in `.claude/rules/trinny-london.md` §3.

## The ask

Owner, 2026-09-14, reading ADR-101 live:

> We designed it so that when you move into the next section, we propose a module approach that compounds. It transitions, but right now it's not really elegant. It jitters and lags a bit, and the way the other parts of the cards unfold, like the full cards, is also not super elegant. It needs to be one smooth move. I also feel that the scroll or the movement to the "We propose a modular approach that compounds" section happens too quickly. Once you enter the studio today, the AI capability card almost immediately starts moving to the next section, which is super annoying and confusing. We need to be more deliberate, and I think we need to delay a bit. Once you move from the studio today and configure it, before you move into the next section, I think all the cards surrounding it (the context, who owns it, where it runs, and where it scales) need to collapse inwards so it feels like one configuration. Only then does it need to move into the next section. The opening or full reveal of the full cards in the "We Propose a Modular Approach" section: right now it's a sort of scan-line effect. It needs to evolve up and down from the upper part of the cards that has moved from the previous section, if that makes sense. […] The text "We Propose a Modular Approach" also needs to be revealed. On the right side, the paragraph needs to be revealed only after all the cards have been revealed. In terms of order, I think the top part, AI capability, should first move to the utter left and then the other card should open up to the right of it. I think that's a more logical flow.

Asked and answered before the cut: the plate's body unrolls DOWN out of a band that lands at the plate's top; the title opens as the chip lands on plate 1; the paragraph after the third plate.

## The diagnosis

Three things, all measurable, none of them taste.

1. **The jitter is a main-thread writer against a compositor scroll.** ADR-101's carrier was posed in DOCUMENT space, from a rAF, while it had to move smoothly relative to a page the compositor was scrolling. Main-thread output lands one frame behind the compositor: this route already measured that on the hero curtain — _"a displacement of exactly one wheel step, every step"_ — and fixed it there by moving the transform onto a scroll timeline. No easing hides a frame of lag. The only cures are compositing the motion or making the frame stationary.
2. **The flight started on the frame the board was seated.** `#proposition` was one viewport and not pinned, so `t = seamProgress(…)` opened on the first pixel past q = 1 — while the arrival's own 1.6 s settle ladder was still running. There was no runway in which to be deliberate.
3. **The plates arrived by a different mechanism than the band.** A time burst (the aperture, on a hysteresis at t ≥ 0.8) opened them mid-flight, so the head band landed on a plate that had appeared by itself.

## The decision: one pinned scene

`#proposition` gets a sticky stage that pins on the SAME frame the record is struck in, and holds for a runway. The phases beat moves INTO the configuration's root and sits absolutely over the board inside that stage. Every object the choreography touches — the board's nodes, the chip, the three head bands, the carrier, the title, the paragraph — is then viewport-STATIONARY while the scene's clock runs. A writer posing a box between two boxes that do not move has nothing to be behind. Everything is a pure, reversible function of one scroll clock; the only fades are monotonic tails; the only burst left on the station is the existing arrival strike, which leaves above the pin.

⚠ **ADR-099's blank frame does not return, and the reason is arithmetic.** That defect was a pin that BEGAN before the record arrived (the record rose while the stage was already stuck). Under ADR-101's 100svh lead, q = 1 IS the station's top at the frame's top — which is exactly the frame a sticky child pins on. The record is struck in and the stage pins on one frame.

### A. The stage, keyed on a stamp

- `#proposition` takes a DEFINITE `height: calc(100svh + var(--tl-scene-runway))` (325svh) and `[data-tl-config-root]` — the slot itself, no prototype change — becomes `position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100svh`. The ancestors permit it (`.station`/`.stations` carry no overflow, transform or filter; the corridor-exit rule lifts `content-visibility` for the whole scene; the turn's stage is the precedent under the same class).
- ⚠ **All of it hangs on `data-tl-scene`, which the writer stamps and which FAILS OPEN.** The stamp is written only once the board, its four role groups, `#phases`, the three plates and their heads have resolved AND `getComputedStyle(slot).position === "sticky"` has been verified; `park()` removes it. Absent — a phone, a short window, reduced motion, a root that never mounted — the station is two flowing beats with the arcs' own seam between them: the inert page, exactly what PRM showed before. A layout that needed the writer to be running would be a page that can go wrong with nothing to say so.
- The phases beat (`TRINNY_PHASES`, split out of `TRINNY_OFFER_SECTIONS`; `TRINNY_SCENE = [board, phases]`) renders in `TrinnyConfiguration`; `TrinnyOffer` opens on the flow with `startIndex` 2. Under the stamp `#phases` is `position: absolute; inset: 0; border-top: 0` (the arcs' `.arc-sec + .arc-sec` seam would rule a hairline across the stage's top). ⚠ **The plates' feet overran the stage by 23.7px at 1280×720** — the plates row overflowed its flowing beat by that much before this pass, harmlessly (a flowing beat grows); inside a one-viewport stage it is the last line of the DELIVERABLE band under the frame's edge for the whole settled dwell. The first cut trimmed `--arc-sec-pad` and claimed 28px back — **a fix that changed nothing** (743.7 measured before and after): the datum seats the head at `start`, content flows from the top, and a bottom pad is never in the sum. The head's own margin is (9vh = 64.8px there), so under `(max-height: 760px)` both heads in the scene take the terminal cut's rung, `clamp(28px, 5.5vh, 72px)` — 39.6px, the feet at 718.5; the taller references were already clear by 42px and more, and nothing changes there. `#offer` keeps `data-corridor-kill`; the drawer's "04 The offer → #offer" is unchanged and correct.
- ⚠ **The plates and the title sit on the coral wash now, not `#offer`'s parchment.** Inherent to the ask: the scene is in `#proposition`, which paints transparent over the turn's ground. Named, not fixed.

### B. The ground on a four-viewport station

`.tl-prop__ground` spans the whole station and its canvas was 100 % × 100 %, redrawn every scroll frame: a ~24-megapixel shader pass per frame. The canvas is viewport-sized and STICKY inside the ground now (`position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100svh`, under the stamp) — the turn's own arrangement inside its stage. ⚠ **`overflow: hidden` came off the ground, and it is not tidiness**: an ancestor with a non-visible overflow is a scroll container, so the sticky canvas would have stuck to the ground's own never-scrolling scrollport — frozen at the ground's top, one viewport above the station, with the frame going bare on the first scroll into the scene and every geometry gate green. The feather is a PAIR now (`vec2 uFeather`, `setFeather(lo, hi)`): a fraction of a viewport-sized stuck canvas would feather the bottom of every frame, so the writer maps the ground's bottom edge into the canvas's own fractions each frame (`feather()`, pure; `TURN_PROP_FEATHER_VH` 0.84 = the old 0.42 of a two-viewport canvas). Solved against the CANVAS's rect, never the viewport's, so the ramp is continuous across the frame the canvas unsticks.

### C. The clock, in viewport units

`sv = clamp(−stationTop / vh, 0, runwayVh)` — how many viewports the station's top has passed the frame's top, published as `--tl-scene` / `data-tl-scene` (2 dp). ⚠ **Not normalised**: a 0 → 1 clock would let a runway edit rescale every window with nothing failing; in viewports a runway edit can only truncate the scene, and `trinny-seam` reads `--tl-scene-runway` out of the sheet and pins `SCENE_END` under it. `sv` joins the writer's delta gate (p and q agree forever after the record lands, which is the whole of the scene's scroll).

⚠ **The fold shrinks before it travels, and it is not a taste choice.** The roles paint seat · layer · card · tools · reach, so the tools and the reach paint OVER the chip: a full-size node sliding to the chip's centre crosses its edge on top of it and reads as going over it, not into it. With the shrink leading (`SCENE_FOLD_SHRINK_END` 0.7 against `SCENE_FOLD_TRAVEL_START` 0.3, both on the node's own eased window), a node is a mark riding its ribbon home by the time it reaches the chip's edge — the unit test walks the board's real geometry (the tools 266 units off, the seat 206) and asserts scale ≤ 0.1 at the crossing.

⚠ **The seat lane retracts the other way.** Three lanes are drawn from the chip outward, so a positive `stroke-dashoffset` retracts their far end toward the chip; the seat's is drawn from the seat DOWN to the chip (`boardLayout`), so it takes `--tl-wire-dir: -1` or it retracts toward the seat — silently, and it reads as a taste decision.

⚠ **The words start late and land early.** A copy is born pixel-identical ON the band it peels off; a decode that began at 0 shuffled that band's own words under the reader before anything had moved — on the still, the real band reading `3G O BBOUT THREE WEEKS`. `decodeClock(e)` holds the source's words until `SCENE_DECODE_START` 0.2 of the travel, and lands them at 0.9 so the hand-over frame is a pure geometry move. One wall per carrier, over both of its lines.

### D. The carrier, inside the stage

`seamCarrier.ts`'s layer is appended INTO the slot, `position: absolute; inset: 0; z-index: 2` (over the two beats' z 1; on the same side of the fixed grit overlay as the chip and the heads, which ADR-101's body-level z 12 was not). Poses are written in the STAGE's coordinates, read off its live rect every frame — ⚠ never assumed to be (0, 0): `.station` is `100vw` with `margin-left: calc(50% − 50vw)`, so with a scrollbar the stage's left is a few px outboard of the frame's. Three carriers, three roles: chip → band 1; band 1 → band 2; band 2 → band 3. `lerpRect` per carrier on its own window — no three-window sum, because there is no seam to bridge. `left/top/width/height`, not `translate`: a transformed box rasterises text on a different sub-pixel grid than the layout-positioned real band, which shows as a one-frame shift at the weld.

⚠ **The layer is mounted from `measure()`, never at effect time.** The stage is a React root's container, and a root's first `render` CLEARS the container before committing its tree — a layer appended at effect time was gone by the time the beats existed, with nothing thrown and the readout saying "no layer". Mounted once the beats have resolved, it is a non-React child React leaves alone.

### E. Two hidden states, and two that are not

- `data-tl-chip="away"` hides the WHOLE card group, outline included. ADR-101 kept the outline as the socket the four ribbons still met; the ribbons have retracted into the chip by the hand-over, so there is no socket to keep.
- Each plate carries `data-tl-plate`: `held` (`visibility: hidden` — nothing of it paints until its band has landed) → `unroll` (the route's clip polygon with the bottom edge at `--tl-unroll-y`, px, from the head's `offsetHeight` to the plate's) → absent. The writer REMOVES the stamp once the plate is whole, so the resting clip is `arcs.css`'s own and the notch guard reads it untouched. The `::before` ring is a child and clips with the plate, so the travelling edge carries no rule — the caption card's own law.
- The board's head and the ledger, and the phases' title and paragraph, open and close on the scrubbed aperture `.arc-ap` already uses for the arcs' media frames — `clip-path: inset(-o calc(50% − ap·(50% + o)))` on the two head COLUMNS (never on `.arc-head`, which carries the arrival's own keyframe animation on the same property), overscanned by `--tl-gl-o` for the chrome the columns hang outside their boxes. Every channel's absent value is 1 = shown.
- ⚠ **`data-tl-scene-past` is a LATCH, not a channel.** A deep reload mid-scene seeds `in` and would run the arrival's settle ladder over nodes the fold has put away; every `animation:` under `[data-tl-prop-arrive="in"]` is scoped `:not([data-tl-scene-past])`. It is set once the withdraw opens and cleared only when the record strikes OUT — cleared on the way back down the scene it re-matches every `:not()`-scoped rule, and a rule that starts matching again RESTARTS its animation: measured, eleven animations running at the pin on the way back up, the whole strike replaying over a board the reader was scrolling back through.

## Traps this pass met, all silent

1. **The observer measures the subtree it watches.** `measureSeam` resolves every token through a probe appended inside the board and the first plate — childList mutations inside the stage, which is exactly what the `MutationObserver` listens for. Left queued they fire the observer, which re-measures, which appends probes: a synchronous microtask loop that never yields to a frame and hangs the tab. `relayout()` drains `mo.takeRecords()` after every measure.
2. **A `ResizeObserver` on the slot is blind once the slot is the stage.** It fires on the mount frame because mounting grows the slot, but the stamped slot is a fixed `100svh`, so from then on nothing about its box changes. The `MutationObserver` is the mount signal; the carrier layer's own per-frame text writes are filtered out of it.
3. **A React root's first render clears its container** (§D).
4. **A latch cleared on the way back replays a burst** (§E).
5. **A decode from zero shuffles the band it is standing on** (§C).
6. **`-top / vh` at the pin is `-0`**, which prints as `-0.00` on the stamp the harness converges on.
