### 3. Properties

**Properties He Praises (TARGET / LIKE):**

- **Centered Architecture:** A layout where the main content and sections are constrained to a central column, rather than stretching across the entire screen.
  - _Seen on:_ Unsiloed, Rollups, Lighthouse, Vite, Arrakis.
  - _Quote:_ "a series of like four websites that actually have a similar structure as our Thoughtform website in the sense that they all have their website and their sections centered."
- **Breathing Room / Spacing:** Generous margins and padding that prevent the design from feeling cluttered or overwhelming.
  - _Seen on:_ Tensorlake, Rollups, CCTP.
  - _Quote:_ "just the spacing and the margin, this just has so much breathing room." / "it's just so easy to look at, everything has breathing room"
- **Pure Vector / Isometric Diagrams:** High-quality, precise visualizations (preferably SVG) used to explain complex systems, rather than relying on videos or generic screenshots.
  - _Seen on:_ Unsiloed, Atomic Tessellator, Augustus.
  - _Quote:_ "exactly the type of thing I want is like these isometric diagrams, visualization, this is fucking great." / "I think videos are less pure than just using SVG or vectors to like really visualize the isometric stuff."
- **Straight Corners (with occasional notches):** A preference for sharp, straight edges on containers and cards, though a single stylized notch is acceptable.
  - _Seen on:_ Unsiloed, Spade.
  - _Quote:_ "Note that they also use like straight corners, they don't have like um rounded corners, which I love. And you could say like hey we have like a a notch, which is fine, but fundamentally we still use straight corners."
- **Clear Flow Visualizations:** Diagrams that clearly articulate a multi-step process or system architecture (e.g., input -> processing -> output).
  - _Seen on:_ Unsiloed (sticky vertical flow), Cluster (horizontal flow).
  - _Quote:_ "the absolute amazing piece is like this. They have like a sticky here and then they basically show their flow."
- **Technical Elegance:** A design that feels engineered and precise, utilizing subtle typography and structured layouts without becoming chaotic.
  - _Seen on:_ Dispatch, Cofounder.
  - _Quote:_ "really feels like a technical website where you also have like fonts, you have like a call to action here"

**Properties He Rejects (DISLIKE):**

- **Full-Width "Anti-Patterns":** Layouts that stretch content across the entire viewport, breaking the constrained, centered feel.
  - _Seen on:_ Canopy Planet.
  - _Quote:_ "It's not full width screen like for example here, this uh Canopy Planet... That's like an anti-pattern maybe I would say, that's not what I want."
- **Overly Stylized "Punch Card" Aesthetics:** Decorative elements that distract from the flow of information.
  - _Seen on:_ Tensorlake (specific left-side components).
  - _Quote:_ "I'm not saying that I want these punch card aesthetics... I don't want that, it is more about the flow."
- **Intrusive Horizontal Dividers:** Lines that cut across the entire screen, disrupting the vertical flow and the left/right rail structure he envisions.
  - _Seen on:_ Rollups.
  - _Quote:_ "they also have a horizontal divider going through it. We don't need that because... that would kind of mess up with like our left and right rail."
- **"Post Stamp" Notches:** Using notches on all four corners of a container, which he finds visually unappealing.
  - _Seen on:_ CCTP.
  - _Quote:_ "I don't like it has like four notches because it feels like a sort of post stamp or some sort of effect, I don't really like that."
- **Chaotic or Overly Dense Layouts:** Designs that pack too much information into a small space without enough structure or tranquility.
  - _Seen on:_ Cluster, Prime Intellect.
  - _Quote:_ "I would say it's a bit more chaotic than I'm that I would like." / "This is more like dense I would say, not necessarily what I want"

### 4. Synthesis

The speaker is looking to refine the visual identity and UX of the "Thoughtform" brand, moving away from previous design proposals that felt chaotic or "Frankenstein" (and which gave him "anxiety").

His core vision is a highly structured, "tranquil," and technical aesthetic. He strongly favors a **centered information architecture**, where content is constrained to a central column rather than bleeding full-width across the screen. This structure must be supported by ample **"breathing room"**—generous spacing and margins that make the site easy to parse.

A critical element of his desired direction is the use of **precise, pure vector visualizations**. He repeatedly praises "isometric diagrams" (specifically mentioning SVG) that clearly articulate complex flows and systems (e.g., showing how tacit knowledge is encoded into a substrate and plugged into an enterprise). He explicitly prefers these crisp, engineered graphics over background videos or generic UI screenshots, which he finds "less pure."

Stylistically, he leans towards sharp, **straight corners** (though he tolerates a single stylized notch) and dislikes overly decorative elements like "punch card aesthetics" or four-corner "post stamp" notches. He wants the design to feel engineered and scalable, drawing heavy inspiration from the clean, diagram-heavy approach of sites like _Unsiloed_ (which he calls a "North Star") and the precise execution seen in _Tensorlake_ (by Hex).

Ultimately, he is asking his team to internalize these principles—centered layouts, breathing room, straight edges, and high-quality isometric flow diagrams—to build modular, scalable variations for Thoughtform's sections, ensuring the final product feels cohesive, technical, and elegant.
