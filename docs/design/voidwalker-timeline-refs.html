Two questions, side by side, on the through-line's real markup and real records. Section 01 answers “the way the dates are placed is really ugly” — five grammars pulled from the design-reference embeddings, each drawn on an actual beat row with the shipped type ladder and spine. 01

Where the date goes

Today the year rides a chip seated on the spine: a bordered box holding a diamond, an ordinal and the year on one line. It exists in that shape for a reason — the opaque ground is what stops the gold rail drawing straight through the type — but it has three problems. It is the busiest object in the lane at the section's smallest type rung; the year is printed twice on story beats (chip and plate); and below 960px it is rotated vertical. Each option below fixes the seating; A4 and A5 also change what the date is for.

One thing worth deciding first, whichever you pick: the //03 ordinal. The casefile deleted every ordinal on that surface (ADR-066, your call) on the grounds that order is carried positionally and the label should be the function alone. This section still prints them. A timeline with real years arguably needs an ordinal even less than a tab rail did — the year is the position. Options A1–A5 all drop it; A0 is the only one that keeps it.

02

The press artifact

The plates below are the real plate box — 16/10, the TR+BL chamfer, the top strip, the fused press bar, and the halftone veil that is already painting over the frame today. The images are real: captured from the five articles on file. Hover any plate to lift the veil, exactly as the live one does.

The capture run answered half the question by itself. Screenshotting the article page gives navigation chrome and a headline on white — that is the "breaks visually from the rest" problem, and it is unfixable by treatment, because the content is a website, not an image. But every one of these articles publishes an og:image: the key visual its own editor chose to be a thumbnail. That is a photograph, and a photograph takes a treatment. Both are shown below on the first row so the difference is visible rather than argued.

Input A — the pageChrome, a logo, a headline, white. Untreatable.This is what "just screenshot the article" means, and it is why the wireframes exist.

Input B — the key visual, no filterA photograph. Compelling, and foreign.The real candidate. No colour treatment here — but the plate's halftone veil is on, because that ships today regardless. Everything below adds to this.

The four treatments

Each shown on the hard case (a bright, colourful stock photo) and the easy one (already warm and dark), because a treatment that only works on the second one is not a treatment.

T1 · Gold duotone + veilThe house recipe, unmodified.The exact chain the tool captures take. The photo stops being foreign colour and becomes the surface's own material — and the halftone is already there.

T1 · Gold duotone + veilThe same recipe on the easy case.Barely a change — this photograph was already in the palette.

T2 · Duotone + CRT scanlineAdds the drifting scanline.The "recorded, real" read — degradation as provenance. Reuses the recipe already in the codebase under the comment "Voidwalker portrait grammar". Dies under reduced motion.

T3 · HolographicScanline plus a raking spectral sheen.Closest to your pager reference. The one option that introduces a colour that is not gold — a real departure, shown so the cost is visible rather than described.

T4 · Hybrid — drawing over evidenceThe wireframe stays; the photo becomes its ground.Photo reads as "real", line-art as "annotation", and the eye never confuses them. Keeps the authored layer that made this surface yours. The boxes here are a schematic stand-in, not the real drawings.

Light theme — parchment printThe same plate, flipped.Photography reads as print on paper. This is the second half of whichever treatment wins, and it needs its own call.

The theme question, stated plainly. There is precedent both ways and they disagree. .arc-card__photo keeps its dark duotone on parchment (no light override at all). .fl-shot__img flips to the parchment-print chain. Whichever you pick, the veil and the press bar are both painted in tokens that do flip — so if the image stays dark, parchment-coloured halftone dots will be printing over a dark photograph. That is the exact defect the hero hit in ADR-058, and it needs a compositing check, not an eyeball.

What I need back

  2. 02The ordinal — does //03 go, the way it went everywhere else?
  3. 03A treatment — T1 … T4, or "keep the wireframes". Note T4 is the only one that keeps the authored drawing.
  4. 04The theme call — does a treated press photo stay dark on parchment, or flip to print?
