## THE TALK (≈15 min)

### 01 · The wake-up call (4:00)

*Let the line glitch in. Wait a beat.*

Within two years, all digital content and products will be absorbed.

I'll show you why I think that, and it isn't an opinion. It's a chart.

### 02 · What it can make (5:00)

*One example at a time. Scroll a step per example; each one lies down in the stack on the left, and the axis of model capabilities grows with it.*

2023: an image. A picture from a prompt, in Midjourney. Strange, and I couldn't stop looking.

Then a film. This is Loop's AI film, running as paid media next to the live-action spots.

Then the tools. Vesper is the image and video tool I built at Loop, for the team.

Then a whole product. This is PhotoCraft: one developer rebuilt Photoshop with Claude, two weeks
ago, and Word, Excel and PowerPoint a week later.

Then worlds. Players mod Halo into new games and worlds of their own.

And mathematics: gold at the International Maths Olympiad, then open problems solved that had stood
for decades.

Every year the stack gets taller, and it climbs because compute does: training compute for frontier
models grows four to five times a year. Rich Sutton called it the bitter lesson in 2019: the general
method that scales with compute and data beats the clever one we built by hand.

### 03 · Intelligence as the interface (9:00)

*One scroll: the grid falls away, the stack slides in layer by layer, and the model and its cords are there as it lands.*

Everything we just saw is the foundation now. It isn't the thing on top any more.

On top of it, the model. Not a brain: an instrument, a machine for thinking.

And from it, these cords go down into every layer: the image, the film, the tool, the product, the
worlds, the maths. That's the new interface. You give it context, hard problems and evals, and it
works every layer below.

Remember PhotoCraft? The point isn't that everyone builds their own Photoshop. Benedict Evans put it
well: nobody's going to vibe-code their own SAP. The point is that people stop opening the other
software. They ask the model, and their agents use the tools for them.

### 04 · Not software (11:30)

*Let the second line land on its own.*

AI can make any software, but isn't software itself.

Sit with that for a second, as a designer. Everything we design for it assumes it's a program.
It isn't.

### 05 · An intelligence (12:30)

*Let the model turn to face the room before you speak.*

So what is it? AI is an intelligence that sits in the middle.

On one side, a tool: it does what you tell it. That's the half routed like a circuit board.

On the other, a collaborator: it thinks it through with you. That's the half that's loose, the
particles going both ways.

And for anyone frowning: this isn't about consciousness or feelings. Every earlier wave of
automation did steps you could write down. This one works wherever there's enough data and you can
check the answer, and nobody knows its limits yet, not even the people building it. It's not
software. It's an intelligence.

### 06 · The crossroads (15:00)

*The head. Left, then right.*

So you have two ways out.

One way: go where AI can't follow. Live action matters more than ever. Cinema is doing well,
festivals sell out, hedge funds are buying them, and at Loop we chase festival deals for a reason.
Real experiences, rooms with people in them. The harder AI tries to look human, the less human it
reads, so don't compete there.

The other way: embrace what this really is. And please, not "UI for agents"; that's reductive. It's
interactions, interfaces and business models for a new digital civilisation, where your agent talks
to theirs. Nobody has designed that yet.

## THE ENDING · the thesis and the Arc (≈4 min)

### The thesis (17:00)

*The corridor opens: "AI sits somewhere between tool and collaborator."*

Sometimes you tell it exactly what to do, like a tool. Sometimes you explain what you're after and
let it work it out, like a collaborator. The real question isn't which model. It's where and how
this new kind of intelligence should take part in the work.

### Navigate · Encode · Build (18:00)

*One line per station.*

That's how I work now. Navigate: learn how it behaves before you hand it work. Encode: write down
what your team knows and what good looks like; the models are smart enough, they lack your context.
Build: give each piece of work its own setup, with people steering.
