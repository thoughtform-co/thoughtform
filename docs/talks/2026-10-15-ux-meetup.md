## THE TALK (≈15 min)

### 01 · The wake-up call (4:00)

*Let the line glitch in. Wait a beat.*

Within two years, all digital content and products will be absorbed.

I'll show you why I think that, and it isn't an opinion. It's a chart.

### 02 · The proof · Digital content (5:00)

*The timeline. Point at the blocks, left to right.*

This is digital content, from 2023 to now. Each block is as tall as the task an agent could finish
that month; METR measured that it doubles about every seven months. On a log scale that's a straight
line, so the blocks climb.

My first Midjourney image, 2023. February 2024, Sora: a minute of video. May 2025, Veo 3: video with
its own sound. And at Loop, AI films running as paid media, next to the live-action spots.

*Point at the faint lane behind.* And there's a second lane behind it. Hold on.

### 03 · The proof · Digital products (7:00)

*The second lane draws in.*

Same floor, digital products. March 2023, the GPT-4 livestream: a sketch on paper becomes a working
website. February 2025, Claude Code: an agent in the terminal. July 2025, gold at the International
Maths Olympiad. And two weeks ago, one developer rebuilt Photoshop in Rust with Claude and put it
on GitHub; sixty thousand stars in two weeks, then Word, Excel and PowerPoint a week later.

*Point at the gold line.*

Why does everything climb at the same rate? That gold rail. Training compute for frontier models
grows four to five times a year. Rich Sutton called it the bitter lesson in 2019: every time, the
general method that scales with compute and data beats the clever one we built by hand. That's not
a philosophy. You're looking at it.

### 04 · Absorbed (9:30)

*The dots cross to the machine and stream in. Let them run before you talk.*

So where does it all go? Into this. The frontier labs, as a machine. Content on one side, products on
the other, and both feed the same model.

Remember PhotoCraft? The point isn't that everyone will build their own Photoshop. Benedict Evans put
it well: nobody's going to vibe-code their own SAP. The point is that people stop opening the other
software. They ask the model, and their agents use the tools for them. The labs become the interface.

### 05 · A voice (11:30)

*Read it slowly.*

"I don't want to use your agent. I want my agent to use your tool."

That's where interfaces are going. Sit with that for a second, as a designer.

### 06 · An intelligence (12:30)

*The stair, then the cloud.*

Here's why it's different from every tool we've had. Evans again: every earlier wave of automation,
mainframes, spreadsheets, software, automated steps you could write down. That's the stair on the
left. This one works wherever there's enough data and you can check the answer. A logic problem
becomes a statistics problem. That's the cloud, and the gold line through it.

And for anyone frowning: this isn't about consciousness or feelings. It's that nobody knows its
limits yet, not even the people building it. That's the title. It's not software. It's an
intelligence.

### 07 · The crossroads (15:00)

*The head. Left, then right.*

So you have two ways out.

One way: go where AI can't follow. Live action matters more than ever. Cinema is doing well,
festivals sell out, hedge funds are buying them, and at Loop we chase festival deals for a reason.
Real experiences, rooms with people in them. The harder AI tries to look human, the less human it
reads, so don't compete there.

The other way: embrace what this really is. And please, not "UI for agents"; that's reductive. It's
interactions, interfaces and business models for a new digital civilisation, where your agent talks
to theirs. Nobody has designed that yet.

## THE ENDING · the thesis and the Arc (≈4 min)

### The thesis (17:00)

*The corridor opens: "AI sits somewhere between tool and collaborator."*

Sometimes you tell it exactly what to do, like a tool. Sometimes you explain what you're after and
let it work it out, like a collaborator. The real question isn't which model. It's where and how
this new kind of intelligence should take part in the work.

### Navigate · Encode · Build (18:00)

*One line per station.*

That's how I work now. Navigate: learn how it behaves before you hand it work. Encode: write down
what your team knows and what good looks like; the models are smart enough, they lack your context.
Build: give each piece of work its own setup, with people steering.

### The proof (20:00)

*The Loop cards. Point, don't read.*

At Loop that's AI films on paid media, four tools the teams own, and a studio where every designer

If you build an interface for a reader that isn't a person, send it to me. Thanks.
